In the previous chapter, you assessed how well selected areas of your Node data management strategy are currently developed. You identified what is working, where practices differ and where there may be room for improvement.

In this chapter, you will use those results to decide where your Node wants to go, what role the Node should play and which areas should be developed further.

## From assessment to direction

Your assessment shows where you are now. It does not tell you where you need to go.

A higher maturity level is not automatically the goal. What makes sense depends on your Node context, mandate, needs and available resources.

Sometimes the next step is to develop something new. In other cases, the better choice may be to connect existing activities, make support easier to find or leave an area as it is.

{% include callout.html type="important" content="Use the maturity model to inform your choices, not to set them. The aim is to decide what makes sense for your Node." %}

## Exercise: define a desired direction

Start with the assessment you completed in Chapter 4. Look at the current situation, what is already working and the possible improvements you identified.

Think about what you would like to be different. What would make a practical difference? What should stay as it is? What would good enough look like for your Node?

Capture your first direction below.

| Strategy area | Current situation | Desired direction | Why does this matter? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Add an area from your assessment | Summarise where you are now | Describe what you would like to be different | Explain why this would help |

{% include callout.html type="tip" content="Keep the desired direction practical. You do not need to define actions or deadlines yet. That comes in the next chapter." %}

## Decide where the Node should act

An ELIXIR Node does not need to provide everything itself. Services, expertise and responsibilities may already sit with partner institutions, national infrastructures, ELIXIR Platforms or other organisations.

The question is where Node level involvement adds value.

A direction might therefore sit at:
* **Node level**, when the Node should lead or coordinate
* **Institutional level**, when individual organisations are best placed to act
* **Shared level**, when Node and institutional roles need to connect
* **National or external level**, when another organisation has the mandate or expertise
* **Out of scope**, when the Node does not need to take responsibility

For example, individual institutions may provide RDM training while the Node helps make existing training easier to find across the network.

{% include callout.html type="note" content="Do not move an activity to Node level simply because the assessment identified room for improvement. First check who is best placed to address it." %}

## Clarify responsibility and decision making

Once the direction is clearer, connect it back to the governance and responsibilities you mapped in Chapter 2.

Some choices can be agreed within the working group. Others may affect funding, services or responsibilities and need agreement from a Node board, steering group, partner institution or another body.

Check:
* who has the mandate to decide
* who needs to be involved or consulted
* whether institutional or national decisions are also needed
* who will own the agreed direction

Use the table to bring these choices together.

| Strategy area | Desired direction | Level | Node role | Decision or owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Add your selected area | Add the desired direction | Node, institutional, shared, external or out of scope | Describe the Node contribution | Note who decides or owns the direction |

If the decision route is unclear, record that too. Clarifying who can decide may itself be an important part of developing the strategy.

{% include callout.html type="tip" content="Make the decision route clear before developing a detailed plan. This helps avoid creating actions that do not yet have the necessary support or mandate." %}

## Exercise: identify your priorities

By this point, you may have several sensible directions. You are unlikely to be able to take all of them forward at once.

Focus on the areas where there is a clear need, where the Node can add value and where there is enough mandate and capacity to make progress.

It may help to check whether the direction:
* addresses an important or recurring need
* fits the Node mandate
* adds something that is not already covered elsewhere
* has enough support, expertise or capacity behind it
* depends on another decision or resource
* can wait without creating a significant problem

Select a small number of priorities.

| Priority | Desired direction | Node role | Decision or owner | Why now? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Add a priority | Describe what you want to achieve | Describe the Node contribution | Note who decides or owns the direction | Explain why this matters now |

{% include callout.html type="tip" content="A short list of realistic priorities is more useful than a long list that cannot be acted on." %}

## What your result could look like

Imagine that your assessment showed that data stewardship expertise exists across the Node, but roles and capacity are difficult to find.

| Strategy area | Desired direction | Level | Node role | Decision or owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Human capacity | Make existing data stewardship expertise and responsibilities easier to find across the Node | Shared | Create a shared overview and connect existing expertise | Node coordination, with input from partner institutions |

This does not require the Node to create a new data stewardship service. The priority is to make better use of expertise that already exists.

Another area may not become a priority at all. If an activity is already well organised by institutions or a national infrastructure, the Node may only need to maintain the connection.

## Check your priorities

Take one final look at the priorities together.

Do they reflect the needs and context you identified earlier? Are they within the Node mandate? Is the Node adding something useful rather than duplicating existing work?

Also check whether responsibility is clear, the right people recognise the priorities and the list is small enough to be realistic. Adjust where needed.

{% include callout.html type="note" content="Priorities can change. Your strategy should provide direction while leaving room to respond when needs or circumstances change." %}

## What’s next

You have defined a desired direction for selected areas, clarified what role the Node should play, considered how decisions will be made and identified which priorities you want to take forward.

In the next chapter, you will turn these priorities into a practical plan.
